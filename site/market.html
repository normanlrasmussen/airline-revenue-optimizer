WHERE WE START

Single-flight inventory control under uncertain demand.

The first research wedge is intentionally narrower than “airline pricing.” We will model one economy-cabin flight, multiple willingness-to-pay segments, a finite booking horizon, and the decision to sell capacity now or protect it for potentially higher-value demand later.

Why this scope? It contains the core economics of revenue management—perishable capacity, uncertain future demand, heterogeneous willingness to pay, and opportunity cost—without immediately hiding the logic inside a huge network model.
THE BUSINESS PROBLEM

A seat has a clock attached to it.

A seat on a scheduled departure cannot be stored. If it leaves empty, its value becomes zero. But selling it too early to a low-fare customer can also destroy value if a higher-fare customer arrives later. Revenue management is the discipline of assigning an opportunity cost to remaining capacity and controlling which demand is allowed to consume it.

  • Capacity is fixed: the aircraft has a finite number of seats.
  • Inventory is perishable: unsold departure capacity disappears.
  • Demand is stochastic: future booking volume and mix are unknown.
  • Customers differ: willingness to pay and booking timing vary.
  • The decision is sequential: every acceptance changes future options.
CORE DECISION

Do we sell this seat now?

For a request with fare f, compare the immediate revenue to the expected future opportunity cost of consuming one unit of capacity.

accept if
fare ≥ marginal value of capacity

Littlewood's rule, EMSR, dynamic programming, and bid-price control are increasingly sophisticated ways to estimate that marginal value.

WHAT WE WILL MODEL

Observed market layer vs. generated booking layer.

OBSERVED

Market evidence

Route-level fares, passenger volume, carrier mix, itinerary structure, seasonality, and broad purchase-window behavior from public data.

GENERATED

Booking process

Day-by-day arrivals, fare-segment demand, cancellations, no-shows, and customer choices that are not directly observable in DB1C.

CONTROLLED

RM policy

Which requests are accepted, what capacity is protected, and eventually how price or offers change as departure approaches.

FIRST MATHEMATICAL ROADMAP

Build sophistication in layers.

  • Baseline: open all inventory / first-come-first-served.
  • Deterministic allocation: known demand, maximize fare × seats.
  • Littlewood: two-fare stochastic protection.
  • EMSR-b: multiple fare classes with nested protection levels.
  • Finite-horizon DP: value capacity dynamically over time.
  • Bid-price control: generalize capacity value toward network RM.
NOT YET

Problems we should postpone.

Network O&D controls, customer-choice assortment optimization, overbooking, continuous pricing, dynamic bundles, and reinforcement learning are important—but they should come after the single-flight simulator is validated. Otherwise the project becomes complicated before it becomes correct.

Portfolio principle: every algorithm should be testable against the same digital-twin demand stream, so improvement is measured rather than asserted.
