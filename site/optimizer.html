METHODS

Math is available when you want it.

The product leads with business outcomes; these sections expose the assumptions and mathematical guarantees behind each method.

EMSR-b: protection levels

Fare classes are sorted from highest to lowest fare. At each fare boundary, higher-fare demand is aggregated into one distribution with a demand-weighted average fare. The protection level is the critical fractile of that aggregate demand distribution. AeroYield models class demand as independent Poisson demand, then uses the standard normal approximation for the EMSR-b quantile.

Guarantee: EMSR-b is a heuristic; it is fast and interpretable but is not generally optimal.

Dynamic programming: optimal sequential control for this model

State is time and remaining seats. For each possible request, the Bellman recursion compares rejecting the request with accepting its fare plus the continuation value with one fewer seat. A request is accepted when its fare is at least the DP bid price: V(t+1,c) − V(t+1,c−1).

Guarantee: the DP is optimal for the discretized model used here: four booking opportunities per day, at most one request per opportunity, known class-specific arrival probabilities, and no cancellations or overbooking.

Clairvoyant benchmark: perfect future information

The benchmark sorts the realized booking requests by fare and fills the aircraft with the highest fares. It is not deployable because it sees the future. Its only purpose is to show an upper bound and quantify each real policy's regret.
