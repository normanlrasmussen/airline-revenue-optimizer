METHODS

Math is available when you want it.

The controls deliberately separate what the policy forecasts from what actually occurs in the simulation.

EMSR-b: protection levels under an imperfect forecast

Fare classes are sorted from highest to lowest fare. Higher-fare forecast demand is aggregated and converted into nested protection levels. The realized simulation then perturbs both demand level and booking timing, so EMSR-b no longer receives the exact stochastic process that generates the requests.

Guarantee: EMSR-b remains a heuristic; forecast error, party sizes, cancellations, and overbooking can all move realized performance away from its planning assumptions.

Dynamic programming: exact forecast model, approximate real-world policy

The DP state remains booking period and remaining booking capacity. Its Bellman recursion is solved exactly for the baseline one-seat forecast model. During simulation, however, realized demand rates and timing are perturbed, requests can contain multiple seats, cancellations can release capacity, and the aircraft can be overbooked. Multi-seat requests are evaluated against the sum of the relevant forecast seat bid prices.

Guarantee: the DP is exact only for its internal forecast model. It is intentionally not given the true realized demand process, so the deployed simulation is a robustness experiment rather than a perfect-model test.

Oracle: perfect future information upper bound

The oracle knows the realized demand and which requests will cancel. It treats cancellations and final seat use with perfect information and relaxes transient booking-limit constraints, so it is an intentionally unattainable upper bound rather than a deployable policy.
